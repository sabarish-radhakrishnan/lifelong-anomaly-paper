Latent Cluster Separation as a Regularizer for Lifelong Anomaly Detection

What actually improves a lifelong anomaly detector: learning a more structured representation, or changing how anomalies are scored? We test the two design choices separately.

01 / The question

Why this study?

AI security systems face a difficult problem: when they learn to recognize new threats, they can become worse at detecting older ones. Some methods try to prevent this by clustering a model’s internal representations and measuring how far new inputs sit from those clusters. But when both changes are introduced together, it is hard to tell which one actually helps.

This study isolates those choices within a variational-autoencoder-based lifelong anomaly detector. Across three intrusion-detection benchmarks, it finds that latent cluster separation can improve performance as a training-time regularizer without replacing reconstruction scoring. Changing to centroid-distance scoring can introduce instability. Crucially, the better scoring rule depends on the dataset.

Central question. Is the benefit from how the model learns, or from how it scores anomalies after learning?

02 / Study design

Separate the components.
Measure the difference.

The study tests two normally bundled design choices through controlled comparisons within a VAE-based lifelong anomaly detector. The goal is to isolate the contribution of latent cluster separation from the effect of changing the scoring mechanism.

01

Baseline

Original VAE-based configuration, without the added latent cluster-separation regularizer; reconstruction-based anomaly scoring.

02

LatentSep-Recon

Introduces the latent cluster-separation objective during training while retaining reconstruction-error anomaly scoring.

03

LatentSep-Dist

Uses the same type of separation objective but switches anomaly scoring to distance from the nearest latent centroid.

03 / Experimental evidence

Key findings

All figures and numerical summaries come from the submitted manuscript. There is no single universally superior scoring method across all three datasets.

ADFA-LD

Regularization can help without changing the scorer.

LatentSep-Recon improves detection quality and backward transfer relative to Baseline in this benchmark, with lower seed-to-seed variance than the distance-scoring alternative.

NSL-KDD

The scoring choice affects reliability.

Reconstruction-based scoring performs better than centroid-distance scoring in the controlled comparison, although neither centroid-based configuration exceeds Baseline overall on this dataset.

UNSW-NB15

The dataset can reverse the result.

Distance-based scoring performs better in the study's comparisons on this dataset, demonstrating why the choice cannot be generalized uniformly across anomaly-detection tasks.

Mechanism insight: A latent-space collapse diagnostic helps explain why distance-only scoring may become unstable, while reconstruction scoring can retain signal by directly comparing model output against the input.
